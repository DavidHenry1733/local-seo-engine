What Travel Vaccinations Are

Travel vaccinations are destination-based immunisations and travel health advice delivered through a structured pharmacy consultation. The pharmacist reviews where you are going, how long you will stay, and what you plan to do, then discusses vaccines and travel-health precautions that may be appropriate for you.

In England, many travel vaccines are provided privately. Some vaccines may be available free on the NHS through a GP after a travel risk assessment — pharmacy availability of any NHS travel vaccines must be confirmed locally. Private travel health consultations with destination-based vaccine recommendations and personal vaccination records. Private consultation and vaccine fees: Private consultation and vaccine fees confirmed at booking.

A pharmacy travel consultation does not guarantee every vaccine is in stock, that every destination requirement can be met same day, or that entry rules will remain unchanged. Official destination guidance can change — patients should still check TravelHealthPro / NaTHNaC and embassy advice for their itinerary.

Suitability for any vaccine or medicine is decided after individual assessment. This service does not replace specialist travel clinics for complex medical histories where referral is safer.

Service information

Who The Service May Be Suitable For

Travel vaccinations may be suitable if you are planning an overseas trip and want destination-based advice before you leave. Common reasons include holidays, business travel, visiting friends and relatives, study placements, volunteering, and adventure itineraries.

You may particularly benefit from an early consultation if:

  • You are travelling to destinations with vaccine-preventable disease risk
  • You need time for multi-dose vaccine courses before departure
  • You are unsure which vaccines or malaria precautions apply to your trip
  • You want a private consultation with clear next steps and documentation

The service may not be suitable, or may need referral, if you are acutely unwell, have complex immunosuppression without specialist input, are pregnant with a complicated travel plan that needs obstetric or specialist advice, or need vaccines the pharmacy cannot supply or is not authorised to give. Yellow fever certificates can only be issued by NaTHNaC-designated centres.

Children, older adults, and people with long-term conditions can often be assessed in pharmacy, but the pharmacist will explain when GP or specialist review is the safer route.

Service information

Planning Your Trip

Book as early as you can. Some vaccines need more than one dose, and protection is more reliable when there is time to complete the course before travel. Last-minute appointments can still help, but options may be limited.

Bring:

  • Your full itinerary, including countries, regions, and travel dates
  • Any previous vaccination records you have
  • A list of current medicines and allergies
  • Details of pregnancy, breastfeeding, or long-term conditions if relevant
  • Your planned activities — for example rural travel, trekking, or animal contact

The pharmacist uses this information to discuss risk and timing. They will not invent a vaccine list that is not supported by your itinerary and clinical assessment.

How The Travel Health Consultation Works

Contact the pharmacy by phone on 0113 275 2103 or via Call 0113 275 2103 to book to ask about appointments. Opening hours: 0900 - 1800.

You will usually be seen in a private consultation room. The pharmacist explains what the consultation covers, including which elements are private and any fees that apply before vaccines are given.

The consultation typically includes:

  1. Review of destination, duration, season, and activities
  2. Medical and vaccination history
  3. Discussion of recommended vaccines and non-vaccine precautions
  4. Shared decision-making about what you wish to proceed with today
  5. Vaccination where clinically appropriate, authorised, and available
  6. Documentation and advice on follow-up doses if needed

Consultation length varies with itinerary complexity. Follow-up visits may be needed for multi-dose courses. Exact duration is confirmed locally and is not guaranteed as a fixed appointment length.

Vaccinations And Travel Health Support Available

Vaccine availability and destination coverage depend on stock, authorisations, and clinical assessment at the time of booking. Do not assume a specific vaccine, price, or certificate is available unless the pharmacy confirms it for your appointment.

Where supported by the pharmacy, travel health support may include:

  • Destination-based vaccine recommendations
  • Administration of selected travel vaccines
  • Advice on insect bite avoidance, food and water precautions, and general travel health
  • Malaria advice and, where authorised, private antimalarial supply
  • Documentation of vaccines given; certificates only where the pharmacy is authorised to issue them

What To Bring And How To Prepare

Arrive with your itinerary and vaccination history if available. Wear clothing that allows easy access to the upper arm for injections. Tell the pharmacist if you feel unwell on the day — vaccination may need to be deferred.

If you take medicines that affect immunity, or if you have had a severe reaction to a vaccine before, say so at the start of the consultation. Bring your GP details if you want records shared.

Service information

Trust And Safety

Pharmacists are GPhC-registered healthcare professionals with a duty to protect patient safety. Vaccines are only given after assessment, informed consent, and confirmation that supply and authorisation allow it.

If your needs fall outside pharmacy scope — for example complex immunosuppression, uncertain vaccine history that needs specialist input, or certificate requirements the pharmacy cannot meet — the team will say so clearly and signpost the right route.

Clinical suitability is always individual. No vaccine or medicine is guaranteed as suitable before consultation.
