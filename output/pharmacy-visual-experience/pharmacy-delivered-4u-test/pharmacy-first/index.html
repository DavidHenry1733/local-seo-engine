SERVICE OVERVIEW

Service information

Pharmacy First is an NHS advanced service in England that allows patients to access assessment and treatment for seven common conditions directly from a participating community pharmacy, without needing a GP appointment first. Trained pharmacists use approved Patient Group Directions (PGDs) and clinical assessment tools to decide whether treatment can be supplied in the pharmacy, whether the patient should be referred to a GP, or whether urgent or emergency care is needed.

The service covers: acute sore throat (including suspected streptococcal infection), acute otitis media (earache) in children and young people, impetigo, infected insect bites and stings, shingles, acute sinusitis, and uncomplicated urinary tract infection (UTI) in women aged 16–64. Consultations take place in a private consultation room. Where treatment is appropriate, the pharmacist can supply prescription-only medicines (including antibiotics where indicated by pathway) at NHS expense, with no prescription charge for eligible NHS patients.

Pharmacy First sits alongside other NHS community pharmacy services (New Medicine Service, contraception, blood pressure checks, etc.) and is designed to reduce pressure on general practice and urgent care while keeping care close to where people live.

Many presentations to GP surgeries and minor injury units are for self-limiting or straightforward infections that can be safely assessed and treated in primary care with the right clinical tools. Pharmacy First formalises pharmacist-led assessment so patients receive timely treatment, appropriate antibiotic stewardship is applied via pathway criteria, and general practice capacity is preserved for more complex care.

The service also addresses access: pharmacies often offer longer opening hours, walk-in availability, and locations in high streets and neighbourhoods where appointment waits for GP care can be lengthy.

CONDITIONS COVERED

Acute Sore Throat (including suspected streptococcal infection)

Acute sore throat is inflammation of the pharynx, usually viral. A subset is caused by Group A Streptococcus (GAS), which may benefit from antibiotic treatment to shorten symptoms and reduce complications in selected patients.

Acute Otitis Media (Earache)

Acute otitis media (AOM) is infection of the middle ear, common in children. It often follows upper respiratory infection.

Impetigo

Impetigo is a superficial bacterial skin infection, usually caused by Staphylococcus aureus or Streptococcus pyogenes. It presents as golden crusted lesions, often around nose, mouth, or on exposed skin.

Infected Insect Bites and Stings

Most insect bites cause local inflammatory reaction. Infection occurs when bacteria enter broken skin.

Shingles (Herpes Zoster)

Shingles is reactivation of varicella-zoster virus in a dermatomal distribution. Early antiviral treatment within 72 hours of rash onset can reduce severity and complications in eligible patients.

Acute Sinusitis

Acute sinusitis is inflammation of paranasal sinuses, usually following viral upper respiratory infection. Most cases resolve without antibiotics.

Uncomplicated Urinary Tract Infection (Women 16–64)

Uncomplicated lower UTI (cystitis) in non-pregnant women aged 16–64 is commonly caused by E. coli.

ELIGIBILITY

The pharmacist will check NHS pathway criteria before treatment is supplied.

NHS eligibility

  • Patient must be registered with an NHS GP in England (or otherwise eligible for NHS primary care services).
  • Consultation must be for one of the seven Pharmacy First conditions.
  • Patient must present in person at a participating pharmacy (unless local policy allows video triage ahead of attendance — attendance still required for supply under PGD in most cases).
  • Pharmacist must confirm identity and eligibility; carers may attend on behalf of children within age rules.

Exclusions

  • Patients outside age bands for the specific condition.
  • Pregnancy (for UTI and some other pathways — refer to GP/midwife).
  • Immunocompromised patients (defined in PGD — often GP referral).
  • Signs of serious complication or systemic sepsis.
  • Conditions outside the seven pathways (e.g. chest infection, conjunctivitis unless separate service).
  • Patients already on conflicting medication or with allergy to first-line pathway treatment without suitable alternative in PGD.
  • Suspected non-pathway diagnosis (e.g. Lyme disease, quinsy, pyelonephritis).

Special circumstances

  • Pregnancy: UTI in pregnancy requires GP/midwife — not Pharmacy First. Other conditions in pregnancy generally referred to GP.
  • Immunocompromised: HIV, chemotherapy, high-dose steroids, etc. — usually GP or specialist referral; shingles and infections may need urgent assessment.
  • Breastfeeding: Some antibiotics compatible; pharmacist assesses per PGD and BNF; complex cases referred.
  • Allergy: Penicillin allergy history assessed; alternative agents per pathway where available.
  • Repeat episodes: Recurrent UTI, recurrent impetigo, recurrent sore throat — may exceed PGD and need GP investigation.
  • Children: Parent/carer consent required; age limits strictly applied per condition.
  • Care homes: Eligible if patient meets criteria; documentation and liaison with care staff may apply.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

Myth: Pharmacy First is the same as asking the pharmacist for advice over the counter.

Reality: It is a formal NHS clinical service with structured assessment, documentation, and PGD-supply of prescription-only medicines where criteria are met.

Trust & credentials

TRUST CONTENT

Every pharmacist supplying medicines under Pharmacy First is registered with the General Pharmaceutical Council (GPhC), the independent regulator for pharmacy professionals in Great Britain. Registration requires a Master of Pharmacy degree, pre-registration training, and passing a registration assessment. Registrants must meet continuing professional development requirements and adhere to Standards for Pharmacy Professionals covering person-centred care, privacy, honesty, and teamwork.

The GPhC inspects registered pharmacy premises against standards including governance, staff training, and suitability of consultation facilities. Patients can verify pharmacist registration on the GPhC online register. Fitness to practise proceedings hold pharmacists accountable for serious misconduct or incompetence. Professional indemnity insurance is mandatory for practising pharmacists.

Pharmacy First delivery requires additional NHS-mandated training beyond baseline registration, ensuring competence in clinical assessment, PGD use, and referral decision-making. The GPhC expects pharmacists to recognise limits of competence and refer when presentations fall outside their scope — this is a professional duty, not optional.
