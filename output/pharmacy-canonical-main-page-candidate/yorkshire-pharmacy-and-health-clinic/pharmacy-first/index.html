What Pharmacy First is

Pharmacy First is an NHS community pharmacy service for some common minor illnesses. Contact the pharmacy to confirm how Pharmacy First is currently arranged. The pharmacy team will ask appropriate questions. Suitability and next steps are determined individually.

Conditions Pharmacy First may cover

Pharmacy First covers defined pathway conditions such as sore throat, earache, impetigo, infected insect bites, shingles, sinusitis, and uncomplicated UTI in eligible women. The pharmacist confirms what can be assessed for you on the day.

  • Sore throat
  • Earache
  • Impetigo
  • Infected insect bites
  • Shingles
  • Sinusitis
  • Uncomplicated urinary tract infection

The conversation is shaped around your symptoms and day-to-day context. The team will ask what you have already tried and which medicines you take. Where the service is not appropriate, you will be advised where to seek further help.

Share a medicines list

Questions about timing and medicines

When you contact the pharmacy, expect questions about symptom timing, medicines already tried, allergies and any warning signs. The team then explains whether Pharmacy First may be suitable. If it is not, you will be advised where to seek further help.

When to contact a GP, NHS 111 or emergency services

Contact your GP, NHS 111, or urgent care if symptoms keep worsening or suggest a more serious infection. Seek urgent medical care for breathing difficulties, chest pain, severe dehydration, confusion, a non-blanching rash, or any emergency symptoms. If you are unsure after symptoms start, contact the pharmacy to ask whether Pharmacy First is the right enquiry.

Pharmacy trust and consultation information

Pharmacy First complements GP care. It does not replace medical review for complex, severe or persistent symptoms. Where the service is not appropriate, you will be advised where to seek further help.

Frequently asked questions

What is Pharmacy First?

It is an NHS service delivered in community pharmacy for some common minor illnesses. The team can explain how it is currently run when you get in touch.

How do I arrange Pharmacy First?

Get in touch with the pharmacy. Staff will explain the current arrangement and ask the questions they need.

What happens when I contact the pharmacy?

Expect a conversation about your symptoms and the medicines you take. What happens next depends on your own circumstances.

What if Pharmacy First is not appropriate for my symptoms?

The team will tell you who to speak to next. That may include your GP, NHS 111 or urgent care if that is safer.

What should I have ready when I get in touch?

A list of your medicines, when symptoms began, and any allergies. Mention pregnancy or breastfeeding if they apply.

Can children use Pharmacy First?

Age limits can apply. Ask the pharmacy team whether this service may be suitable for a child.

Does Pharmacy First replace my GP?

No. It sits alongside GP care for some common minor illnesses. Ongoing and complex conditions stay with your GP.

Is the conversation confidential?

Yes, pharmacy advice is confidential. Ask how a private conversation is currently set up.

What if I am already seeing my GP for the same problem?

Mention this when you get in touch. Issues already under GP review often need medical assessment instead.

Can I use Pharmacy First for any illness?

No. Only some common minor illnesses may be considered. The team will confirm whether it may be suitable for you.

What should I do if symptoms get worse?

If symptoms worsen, persist, or include emergency warning signs, use your GP, NHS 111 or urgent care. Do not wait for a pharmacy conversation.
