What Is Pharmacy First?

Broom Lane Pharmacy offers pharmacy first for patients who want pharmacist assessment and clear next steps — here is what the service involves and why people book.

SERVICE OVERVIEW

What Pharmacy First is

Pharmacy First is an NHS advanced service in England that allows patients to access assessment and treatment for seven common conditions directly from a participating community pharmacy, without needing a GP appointment first. Trained pharmacists use approved Patient Group Directions (PGDs) and clinical assessment tools to decide whether treatment can be supplied in Broom Lane Pharmacy, whether the patient should be referred to a GP, or whether urgent or emergency care is needed.

The service covers: acute sore throat (including suspected streptococcal infection), acute otitis media (earache) in children and young people, impetigo, infected insect bites and stings, shingles, acute sinusitis, and uncomplicated urinary tract infection (UTI) in women aged 16–64. Consultations take place in a private consultation room. Where treatment is appropriate, the pharmacist can supply prescription-only medicines (including antibiotics where indicated by pathway) at NHS expense, with no prescription charge for eligible NHS patients.

Pharmacy First sits alongside other NHS community pharmacy services alongside other NHS community pharmacy services and is designed to reduce pressure on general practice and urgent care while keeping care close to where people live.

Why it exists

Many presentations to GP surgeries and minor injury units are for self-limiting or straightforward infections that can be safely assessed and treated in primary care with the right clinical tools. Pharmacy First formalises pharmacist-led assessment so patients receive timely treatment, appropriate antibiotic stewardship is applied via pathway criteria, and general practice capacity is preserved for more complex care.
