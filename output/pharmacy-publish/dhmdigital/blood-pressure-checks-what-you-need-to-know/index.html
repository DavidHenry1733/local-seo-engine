Blood Pressure Checks — What You Need To Know

Before you book blood pressure checks at DHM Digital, here is practical information about preparation, results and when other care routes apply.

Understanding Blood Pressure Readings

Blood pressure is written as two numbers. The first (systolic) is the pressure when your heart beats. The second (diastolic) is the pressure when your heart rests between beats.

High blood pressure rarely causes obvious symptoms. That is why screening matters — a normal result is reassuring, but feeling well does not rule out a raised reading.

One reading on its own — at home, in pharmacy, or at your GP — is not enough to diagnose high blood pressure. Repeated measurement is required, and ABPM is the preferred method to confirm whether readings stay raised throughout the day.

If your reading is higher in a clinical setting than at home, you may have white-coat hypertension — where anxiety in a medical environment temporarily raises the result. ABPM or home monitoring over time helps tell the difference.

When Urgent Medical Review Is Needed

Blood pressure screening is not for emergencies. Call 999 or go to A&E for chest pain, suspected stroke, severe breathing difficulty, collapse, sudden confusion, or any life-threatening symptom — regardless of what a blood pressure reading shows.

Seek same-day medical review if your clinic reading is 180/120 or higher (even without symptoms), if ABPM shows a very high average, if the device detects an irregular pulse that is not already known and investigated, or if your reading is low and you experience regular fainting, falls, or daily fear of fainting.

Call 999 immediately if a very high reading is accompanied by chest pain, severe headache with neurological symptoms, new confusion, signs of heart failure, or acute kidney problems.

The NHS pharmacy service is not appropriate when you need emergency care, when you require daily blood pressure monitoring over a set period, when you are pregnant and need maternity blood pressure assessment, or when you fall outside the service eligibility criteria without a GP referral.

If the service is not suitable, Our pharmacist team tells you where to go next. You should never leave without a clear plan.

Patient Concerns

Concern: A high result means I will be forced onto medication immediately.

A raised reading starts a assessment pathway — not automatic treatment. Your GP considers your overall cardiovascular risk, lifestyle, and confirmed readings before recommending any medicine. Lifestyle changes are often part of the plan.

Concern: I will be judged for smoking, weight, or drinking.

Lifestyle questions help the pharmacist give relevant advice and signpost support services. Consultations are private and professional — the aim is to help, not to criticise.

Concern: Anxiety will make my reading wrong, so the result is useless.

Feeling nervous can temporarily raise a reading. That is a recognised pattern. ABPM exists partly to see what happens outside a clinical setting. Tell the pharmacist if you are anxious — it helps them interpret the result.
